Where entries go.

      
Students’ logs live on their devices. Monitoring means giving each entry a place to land that you can open. There is no server here, so the landing place is a form you own, with a spreadsheet behind it.

      

        
Microsoft Form → Excel in OneDrive (recommended)
Fits MOE staff accounts: your form, your OneDrive, no scripts. The app opens your form with the entry already filled in; the student presses Submit once. Responses land in an Excel workbook in your OneDrive (open it in Excel for the web to refresh). Filter by your email or class code. Limit: 4,000 characters per answer.

        
Google Form → Sheet
Silent submit, no extra tap. Needs the form set to accept responses from outside the organisation (iCON forms default to sign in), which also means the endpoint is public. Fine for a pilot; less good for a whole school.

      

      

        
No drop box
The student copies the entry as text and pastes it into SLS, Classroom, Teams or an email. Zero set up, works today.

        
Exports (any time)
Students export their log as a file and hand it in through an assignment. Drop those files on the Review tab here and read them together. No infrastructure at all.

      

    

    

      
Connect a form to this class

      

        
        
      

      
The thirteen fields. Make one question per field, all “long text” except the first: timestamp, studentName, studentEmail, classCode, teacherEmail, framework, subject, topic, entry, competencies, depth, nextStep, entryId. When you generate the pre-filled link, answer each question with its own key (literally type studentName into the student name question) and paste the link above.

      

      

    

    

      
Set up the Microsoft Form (once, about 15 minutes)

      
    
        
  1. Sign in at forms.office.com with your MOE account (or open Forms from OneDrive so the workbook is created next to it). New form, title it Learning Log drop box: [your name].
    2. 
        
  2. Add thirteen questions in the order above. Use Text with Long answer on for entry, competencies and nextStep; plain text for the rest. Make none of them required except studentEmail and entry.
    3. 
        
  3. Settings: Anyone can respond. Sign in is off, so students on iCON can submit without a Microsoft account. Turn off “Record name”.
    4. 
        
  4. Top right … → Get pre-filled URL → switch on Enable pre-filled answers, type each field key into its own question, then Get pre-filled link. Paste that link above. The mapping table fills itself.
    5. 
        
  5. Responses → Open results in Excel. That workbook lives in your OneDrive and updates when you open it in Excel for the web. Add a filter on teacherEmail or classCode.
    6. 
        
  6. Download the class file on the first tab (the mapping is inside it) and give students the code or link.
    7. 
      

      
Whole school later: one shared form owned by a department account, and a Power Automate flow (Forms trigger → “Add a row into a table”, both standard connectors on an education licence) that routes each response to the right teacher’s workbook by teacherEmail. What this is, in five lines.

      
A personal learning log for Beattyians. Two frameworks from our own slides: GROW by reflecting after a lesson (Gift, Rise, Own, Watch) and ACT on Feedback (Acknowledge, Connect, Test). The guiding questions are quoted verbatim and every entry answers them. A competency lens then asks: which of MOE’s 21st Century Competencies did this stretch? A coach reads the entry: the built in one offline, and optionally Claude in your voice.

      
Everything a student writes stays on their device unless they press Send (to your form) or Export (a file). There is no database and no login; a name, a class code and an optional PIN for shared laptops.

    

    

      
The competency lens

      
MOE’s refreshed framework (2023) names nine Emerging 21CC in three domains, plus five Social-Emotional Competencies. The app uses MOE’s names so that what students see here matches the SLS 21CC Navigator, EAGLES and syllabus tables. Students claim at most two per entry, place themselves on three levels (tried it, getting there, can teach it) and can point to where it shows. The coach then holds the claim against the words, and nudges from task talk (“I finished the worksheet”) to competency talk (“I noticed I was guessing, so I checked the units”).

      

    

    

      
How to run it in a class

      
    
        
  1. Week 1. Give the code or link. Ten minutes in class: everyone writes one GROW entry on today’s lesson. Read two aloud (with permission). Point at the coach’s “answer this now” question.
    2. 
        
  2. Rhythm. One GROW a week after a chosen lesson; one ACT every time a marked piece returns. Short and frequent survives the exam term; long and monthly does not.
    3. 
        
  3. Your reading. Open the Excel workbook (or the Review tab) before the next lesson. You are looking for two things: the next step they wrote, and whether they named a competency with a moment behind it.
    4. 
        
  4. Close the loop. Mention one student’s next step in class, anonymised. Tick yours. Students tick theirs on the Today screen.
    5. 
      

    

    

      
Privacy and safety notes

      
    
        
  • The AI coach never receives the student’s name or email; it receives the entry text, the subject and your class file.
    • 
        
  • Students do not create accounts anywhere. The three main AI providers require account holders to be 18 or older, which is why the relay exists.
    • 
        
  • The PIN is a courtesy lock for shared devices, not security. Reflections can be personal; treat exports as you would a student’s journal.
    • 
        
  • A cleared browser loses the log. Remind students to export at the end of each term; the export imports back into any device.
