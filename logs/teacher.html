    Beatty’s I can statementsDraft

    How a statement is secured. A student links an entry to a statement and points to the sentence that shows it. The coach checks that the words are really there. A statement is secured when at least two backed entries, from two different subjects or CCAs, sit at least two weeks apart.
    How levels are earned. In each competency, a band is met in a strand when at least half its statements, rounded up, are secured. Emerging: the competency shows in an entry, or any statement is secured. Developing: in every strand, the Lower Sec band is met or an Upper Sec statement is secured. Proficient: Developing, and at least one Upper Sec statement secured. Leading: the Upper Sec band met in every strand, and a teacher confirms it. The social and emotional competencies have no statements yet, so they show evidence only.

    A group file is a small JSON document.

    One per class or CCA you teach. It carries your name and email, the subject or activity, your coach voice, your success criteria, and where students’ shared entries should go. Students load it by typing the code (if the file is published in logs/classes/) or by opening the share link you give them. A student can belong to several groups. Nothing here is sent anywhere by this page.

    Where shared entries land.
    
      
    Students’ logs live on their devices, and nothing reaches you until a student shares a section with you and presses Send. The landing place is a form you own, with a spreadsheet behind it; paste its rows into the Inbox to read them here. Without a form, students send you a link in Teams that opens straight in your Inbox.
    
      
    
        
    Microsoft Form → Excel in OneDrive (recommended)
    Fits MOE staff accounts: your form, your OneDrive, no scripts. The app opens your form with the entry already filled in; the student presses Submit once. Responses land in an Excel workbook in your OneDrive (open it in Excel for the web to refresh). Filter by your email or class code. Limit: 4,000 characters per answer.
    
        
    Google Form → Sheet
    Silent submit, no extra tap. Needs the form set to accept responses from outside the organisation (iCON forms default to sign in), which also means the endpoint is public. Fine for a pilot; less good for a whole school.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    No drop box
    The student copies the entry as text and pastes it into SLS, Classroom, Teams or an email. Zero set up, works today.
    
        
    Exports (any time)
    Students export their log as a file and hand it in through an assignment. Drop those files on the Review tab here and read them together. No infrastructure at all.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Connect a form to this class
    
      
    
        
        
      
    
      
    The eighteen fields. Make one question per field, titled with its key: timestamp, studentName, studentEmail, classCode, teacherEmail, framework, subject, topic, entry, competencies, depth, nextStep, entryId, context, section, then last and in this order payload1, payload2, payload3. The three payload answers carry the whole entry, compressed, so the Inbox can rebuild it with the coach’s read. When you generate the pre filled link, answer each question with its own key (literally type studentName into the student name question) and paste the link above. A form made for the Learning Log with thirteen fields still works; add the five new ones to get the full entry in your Inbox.

    What this is
    
      
    An E21CC Log for every Beattyian, from Secondary 1 onwards. It holds journals: GROW by reflecting after any learning moment (a lesson, a training, a camp day) and ACT on Feedback when feedback comes back, with the school’s guiding questions quoted verbatim. A third journal, ASK, is a placeholder until its questions are written. Each entry carries a claim: which of Beatty’s I can statements it shows, and the sentence that shows it. A coach checks the claim against the student’s own words.
    
      
    Home draws MOE’s framework as a wheel: core values at the centre, the five Social and Emotional Competencies around them, the nine emerging competencies outside. Each of the nine is shaded by the student’s level, Emerging, Developing, Proficient or Leading, earned from the statements they secure (see Statements). GROW and ACT entries both count. Leading needs you: a student who meets it shows as waiting, and you confirm it from their shared domain. Journals are organised by the student’s own subjects, CCA and experiences. Once a term, students look across one domain.
    
      
    The log lives on the student’s device. You see a section only when the student opens it to you and presses Send. You reply in the margin.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    How to run it with a class
    
      
      
        
    1. Set up once. Make a group file (Set up). Connect a Microsoft Form if you want entries in Excel; without one, students send you a link in Teams.
      2. 
        
    2. Week 1. Give the code or link. Ten minutes in class: everyone writes one GROW on today’s lesson, then opens Sharing and turns on the sections you ask for (for a subject class, usually GROW and ACT).
      3. 
        
    3. Rhythm. One GROW a week after a chosen lesson or session; one ACT whenever a marked piece returns. Short, regular entries are easier to keep up during exam weeks.
      4. 
        
    4. Read. Paste the new rows into the Inbox before the next lesson. Look for two things: the next step, and whether the claimed competency has a moment behind it.
      5. 
        
    5. Reply. Write a short note in the margin, next to the line it is about, rather than a long comment at the end. Then press Send notes: the link opens in the student’s log, beside the paragraph you wrote on.
      6. 
        
    6. End of term. Ask for a look across one domain. Form teachers: the Class grid shows which domains are still empty.
      7. 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    The competency lens
    
      
    MOE’s refreshed framework (2023) names nine Emerging 21CC in three domains, plus five Social and Emotional Competencies. Students claim at most two I can statements per entry and point to the sentence that shows each.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Privacy and safety notes
    
      
      
        
    • The AI coach never receives the student’s name or email; it receives the entry text, the context and the group file’s voice.
      • 
        
    • Students do not create accounts anywhere. The three main AI providers require account holders to be 18 or older, which is why the school relay holds the key.
      • 
        
    • Share links and note links carry the text itself. Send them only to the person they are for, in Teams or school email. Note links only open in the log of the student whose email they were made for.
      • 
        
    • The PIN is a courtesy lock for shared devices, not security. Reflections can be personal; treat pasted rows and exports as you would a student’s journal, and clear old items from this browser.
      • 
        
    • A cleared browser loses a student’s log. Remind students to export at the end of each term; the export imports back into any device.
