When the PSI exceeds 100, or the 1-hr PM2.5 exceeds 55, visually check on students’ well-being, especially those with pre-existing lung or heart conditions.

If the student or staff is feeling unwell:

  • Pull them out of the activity, alert the parents of the student, and send the student/staff for medical attention if necessary.
  • Those with breathing difficulties are to receive immediate medical attention from nearest GP, polyclinic or hospital.
  • Air-conditioned room(s) (minimally one room) equipped with air purifier(s) should be made available as a temporary holding room while awaiting transport.

Measures

24-hr PSI 100 and below, and 1-hr PM2.5 55 and below.

No band measures apply. Continue to monitor NEA readings.

24-hr PSI 101 – 200 (UNHEALTHY) OR 1-hr PM2.5 56 – 150 (ELEVATED)

Curriculum & student activities:

Students with pre-existing lung or heart conditions / are feeling unwell
Avoid outdoor activities and physical exercise
Students without pre-existing conditions and are feeling well
  • Avoid prolonged outdoor/strenuous activities
  • Indoor non-strenuous physical activities acceptable

Review the location, intensity and duration of physical/outdoor activities.

  • Check if students are well before the start of the activity.
  • Students to be informed that they should alert their teachers if they are not well.
  • Look out for students who appear unwell during the activity

Continue to monitor air quality closely, and make further adjustments to the activities (refer to Additional Guidelines for Activities when Air Quality is “Unhealthy” / “Elevated”).

Air purifiers to be turned on with doors and windows closed.

  • Periodically reopen doors or windows for better ventilation. This could be done naturally when the teacher leaves the classroom at the end of the lesson, or when students go to the toilet.
Staff
  • Minimise prolonged or strenuous outdoor work.
  • N95 masks are not required for indoor and outdoor work.
MOE press conference, 16 Oct 2026
If the 1-hr PM2.5 reading is elevated (56 – 150)
  • Prolonged or strenuous outdoor physical activities will be avoided.
  • Indoor non-strenuous activities such as light physical exercises may continue.
  • Students who are unwell or have pre-existing heart or lung conditions will be excused from these activities.
  • Air purifiers will be deployed in indoor spaces, such as classrooms, labs, school halls, and Indoor Sports Halls.
24-hr PSI 201 – 300 (VERY UNHEALTHY) OR 1-hr PM2.5 151 – 250 (HIGH)

Curriculum & student activities:

Students with pre-existing lung or heart conditions / are feeling unwell
Avoid outdoor activities and indoor physical activities
Students without pre-existing conditions and are feeling well
  • Avoid outdoor activities and strenuous indoor physical activities.
  • Indoor non-strenuous physical activities acceptable
  • Check if students are well before any activity.
  • Minimise after-school activities.
  • Air purifiers to be turned on with doors and windows closed.
    • Periodically reopen doors or windows to provide better ventilation. This could be done naturally when the teacher leaves the classroom at the end of the lesson, or when students go to the toilet.
  • Students to consume their food in an indoor venue during recess.

Dismissal: No early dismissal of students.

However,

  • Allow parents to fetch their children home early if they are concerned about their child’s well-being; and
  • Check that students are asymptomatic (i.e. students do not exhibit/report any symptoms as a result of the haze situation (e.g. coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath)) before dismissing them, especially those with pre-existing conditions.
Staff5
  • Minimise prolonged or strenuous outdoor work. If unavoidable, staff to use N95 mask.
  • N95 masks are not required for indoor work.
MOE press conference, 16 Oct 2026
If the 1-hr PM2.5 reading is high (151 – 250)
  • Students will remain in enclosed indoor spaces with air purifiers.
  • Students will avoid both outdoor activities and strenuous indoor activities, and have their meals indoors.
  • Schools will operate as per normal school hours.
  • Schools will replace outdoor activities (such as CCAs and camps) with indoor activities where possible. If not, the organisers will cancel or postpone them.
24-hr PSI ≥ 301 (HAZARDOUS) OR 1-hr PM2.5 ≥ 251 (VERY HIGH)

If PSI forecast for the following day is ≥301, MOE will consider school closure for students in Primary schools, Secondary schools & Secondary section of IP schools. If schools are to close, schools will implement home-based learning for students to ensure that students’ learning is not disrupted4. To support parents who are unable to make alternative childcare arrangements, schools and SCCs will continue to operate.

FOR JCS, CI AND JC SECTION OF IP SCHOOLS

Curriculum & student activities: Students should be in air-conditioned spaces. Lessons could be modified where appropriate.

Dismissal: Check that students are asymptomatic before they are dismissed from school.

FOR TRANSIENT SPIKES DURING THE SCHOOL DAY

Curriculum & student activities:

  • Scale down lessons where possible till air quality improves and drops below hazardous.
  • All students should be in either air-conditioned rooms or rooms with doors and windows closed with air purifiers deployed.
  • Avoid after-school activities.

Dismissal: No early dismissal of students. However,

  • Allow parents to fetch their children home early if they are concerned about their child’s well-being; and
  • Check that students are asymptomatic before dismissing them, especially those with pre-existing conditions.
  • Encourage parent/caregiver of students with pre-existing conditions to pick them up from school at dismissal time (unless they are taking school bus), especially if the child is in primary school.
Staff5
  • Minimise all outdoor work. If unavoidable, staff to use N95 mask.
  • N95 masks are not required for indoor work.
MOE press conference, 16 Oct 2026
If the 1-hr PM2.5 reading is very high (251 and above)
  • Schools will scale down lessons, by reducing the intensity of indoor activities, and suspend all physical and after-school activities.
If the 24-hr PSI forecast for the following day is in the hazardous range (300 and above)
  • MOE, MSF and ECDA will strongly encourage parents and guardians to keep their infants and young children at home.
  • MOE will consider closing schools and transitioning to home-based learning for younger students from preschool up to secondary level.
  • Kindergarten programmes, Early Intervention Programme for Infants and Children (EIPIC and EIPIC-P) programmes, private programmes, as well as preschool-based early intervention programmes such as the Development Support-Plus programme and Development Support-Learning Support programme, will also be suspended.
To support students whose parents/guardians are unable to make alternative care arrangements
  • Primary and secondary schools (including SPED schools) as well as Student Care Centres and Special Student Care Centres in our schools and SPED schools will remain physically open.

4 For extended school closure, schools may need to activate Full Home-Based Learning. Please refer to SOP 5.1 on Curriculum Continuity during Extended Unscheduled School Closure.

Notes:

  1. Strenuous activity causes your heart rate to increase significantly. Your breathing will get harder and faster and it will be more difficult to hold a conversation. The exercise tolerance of children may vary from child to child, based on their individual physical fitness and health condition. If a child feels unwell, he/she is to stop participating in the activity, be moved indoors (with air purifier) and seek medical attention, as appropriate to the severity of the child’s condition.
    1. Examples of strenuous activities are circuit training, jogging/running, mass PE involving aerobic-type exercises.
    2. Examples of non-strenuous activities are stretching, walking, static practice tasks, e.g., passing/shooting when stationary.
  2. Prolonged is defined as continuous exposure for several hours.

MOE press conference, 16 Oct 2026

MOE and Special Education (SPED) schools continuity plans
  • Available forecasts and advisories, as well as actual ground sitation will be taken into consideration to make an informed decision.
  • School leaders will take into account the 1-hour PM 2.5 readings and exercise judgement in managing school-based learning and activities.
  • Measures will be adjusted where needed to safeguard the health and well-being of our students and staff.
  • If there is a need to move nationally, MOE will make the announcement through our schools and official channels.
Our schools have already been taking precautionary measures
  • Adjusting activities
  • Deploying air purifiers
  • Paying extra attention to students who report to be unwell and/or have pre-existing health conditions
  • Taking practical steps to reduce exposure to haze where necessary
National Examinations
  • Students taking the O- and A-Level exams will sit for their papers in enclosed air-conditioned venues with air purifiers.
  • If they are unwell and unable to take or continue with an exam, they should see a doctor, obtain a medical certificate (MC), and apply for special consideration through their schools. Their schools will guide them on the necessary procedure.
  • Should the 24-hour PSI forecast reach hazardous levels, we will consider rescheduling O-Level papers in line with the health advisory for younger students.
  • The A-Level papers are generally less likely to be affected as the students are older, but we will also take reference from forecast as well as actual ground circumstances in making decisions on whether they will continue.
  • Details of any rescheduling of O- and A-Level papers to the earliest practicable date will be publicly announced by the Singapore Examinations and Assessments Board (SEAB).
  • Schools will also inform students and parents directly if any national exam needs to be rescheduled because of haze conditions.
Junior Colleges, Millenia Institute and Post-Secondary Education Institutions
  • Junior Colleges (including the JC sections of Integrated Programme schools), Millennia Institute, ITE, polytechnics, Autonomous Universities and University of the Arts Singapore, because students are older, will generally continue in-person lessons.
  • We will also take reference from forecasts as well as actual circumstances on the ground in deciding whether to continue in-person activities or what measures should be adopted.
