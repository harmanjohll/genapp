The problem, in three numbers

Underneath these: the block is already streamed — G3 students draw 51 enrichment entries where G1 draw 9 E6 — and 36% of student-facing block time is not enrichment at all E2. Sec 4 and 5 barely feature. The block exists on paper; nobody has defended the hour.

Three assets nobody has spent

The same calendar shows we already own the raw material. None of these cost anything new.

A fortnightly home-based Wednesday

25 HBL days over two years, 19 of them Wednesdays, median gap 14 days in term. The alternation between an on-campus Wednesday and a home one is already running and already accepted by parents. It has just never been used as a design. E14

Five houses, named and coloured

Hood, Jellicoe, Harwood, Sturdee, Fisher — real naval history, colours now confirmed. A house is a standing unit of ~216 Beattyians and ~18 staff that already exists, so work can be handed to it without constituting a single new committee.

An empty midweek

Nationally, Wednesday is the busiest CCA day in Singapore. At Beatty it is nearly the emptiest — 13 CCA occurrences against 29 on our busiest day. The block we want to guard is structurally free. E7

One asset with a warning on it: about 82 days a year already carry an all-day event, mostly examinations E16. Those days are cleared of lessons and staffed. A handful can become designed intensives — but the exam calendar itself is not negotiable, and every possibility below plans around it rather than through it.

Three possibilities

All three share the same foundation: five class-anchored vertical houses, a stable catalogue of activities refreshed rather than reinvented, student leaders doing the running with teachers roaming, and Wednesday for everyone, Thursday for depth — Renzulli's Explore tier universal, a Build tier by choice, a small Pursue tier that turns over so the same seats reach more Beattyians. They differ in exactly one thing: what we do with the Wednesday rhythm.

The road examined and set aside: talent-area houses

We modelled houses as talent areas — join Fisher because you are a STEM Beattyian. It buys the deepest pipeline per field and loses everything else: sorting thirteen-year-olds by aptitude tracks banding so closely that the streaming the audit found would get a name and a colour, and the door closes on every late developer. First moves — the same under all three

These are no-regrets. Whichever possibility the room prefers, these start now.

  1. Publish the examination and oral windows before cycles are set.

    The single biggest cause of lost block days is collision with assessment that was always going to happen. Sequence the calendar the other way round.

  2. Freeze the catalogue.

    Stop inventing. Convert the ~16 programmes that already persist into named house clusters, retire the one-offs, and refresh a quarter a year. ~11 designs instead of 42.

  3. Train the 84.

    Student-led is the only arithmetic that works at 1:108. The training of Sec 3–4 leaders is the deliverable of Term 1, not a nice-to-have — each house trains its own.

  4. Score the Cup per capita, and keep it out of the pathways.

    Per-capita scoring removes house-size advantage. And the moment a house earns points for its members' olympiad results, the talent pool fragments into five — houses steward fields; they never own the door.

  5. Fix the recording.

    246 different ways of writing a time; 991 of 1,380 records with no venue. A time picker and a venue list cost nothing and are the difference between next year's audit and next year's anecdote. E15

The decision — three questions only this room can answer

1 · Do we trust a home Wednesday?

Not "is HBL possible" — we already run it fortnightly. The question is whether we will invest in asynchronous material good enough that a home Wednesday is learning rather than absence. Yes opens Possibilities 2 and 3. No means Possibility 1, honestly chosen.

2 · Are groups of 25 acceptable, or do we need 15?

Renzulli's published cluster range is 5–15, up to 25 only with a second adult. At 25 we are running small classes; at 15 we are running enrichment clusters. Only Possibility 3 reaches 15 without new rooms or new staff.

3 · How much depth for how many?

The Pursue tier at 15% of the cohort with a revolving door reaches roughly 60% of Beattyians at some point in four years, ~30 sessions each. Hold the same seats fixed and the same money reaches 8%. This is a values choice about breadth against depth — the dashboard shows the curve, but the room picks the point.

A recommendation, if one is wanted: adopt Possibility 1 now as the floor, commit the five first moves this term, and set one gate for Possibility 2 — a bank of home-Wednesday tasks that two HODs certify as worth the afternoon. If the gate is passed by Term 3, name the fortnight for next year. Possibility 3 waits for one clean year of 2.
