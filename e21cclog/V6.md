# Version 6: the audit, Harman's decisions, and what is open

11 November 2026. Nothing is built. This page records an audit of the Log (5.2) against Harman's seven intents, his answers that day, and what is still open, so the next chat can start here. Harman's instruction stands: questions before code, and no action until he says so.

## The seven intents (Harman, 11 November)
1. Students reflect on any learning or lived experience: subjects, CCA, camps and beyond.
2. GROW is the frame for metacognition and, to a degree, self regulation. ACT is the frame for acting on feedback. Both serve learning to learn and to live, and habits of learning for life.
3. GROW and ACT can stay at the level of task and process, and can be raised to competencies: MOE's E21CC, already in the app.
4. The I can statements are Beatty's own, and are used as given for now.
5. Teacher feedback is essential. A self report must be validated or affirmed, and advice given. Not another SLS, and no database.
6. In the longer term the Log follows the learner. The word portfolio is not used.
7. As part of a larger project, the Log informs teaching and learning: how students are grouped, what a lesson should focus on.

The Log is one part of a larger aim that includes CoCher (teacher affirmation and advice, data for lesson design and enactment). The two are not to be conflated. Work stays in genapp unless there is a reason to do otherwise.

## The audit, in short
| Intent | Verdict | The gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Any lived experience | Mostly met | Seven entry types exist. `experiences` in `school/beatty.json` is empty. GROW still asks what I "understand", which fits a lesson better than a camp or a setback. Only subject and CCA entries have a teacher to go to (`teacherFor` in `assets/app.js`). |
| 2. GROW and ACT | Strongest part | A teacher's note does not open an ACT, so the two frameworks never meet inside the Log. |
| 3. Task and process, raised to competencies | Met, with a nudge to add (see decision 3) | A student can pass the claim step without a claim and is not nudged. Goals and Calendar are a third centre that the seven intents do not name. |
| 4. I can statements as given | Content met, validation hollow | All 85 statements are there word for word. The check behind "secured" is not sound (finding 1). |
| 5. Teacher feedback | Built, unproven, fragile | Findings 2 and 3. |
| 6. Follows the learner | Format ready, custody not | One portable file, with backup and restore that double nothing. But the log lives in one browser on one device, and nothing yet tells the story across years. |
| 7. Informs teaching | Thinnest | Each console holds only what was sent to that teacher on that device. Nothing gathers what a class says it does not yet understand. |

### Three findings
1. **"Secured" can be earned with almost any sentence.** Students only have the built in coach, which matches keywords for the competency, not the statement. Run directly against the engine (`builtinE21cc`, `linkBacked`, `securedOf` in `assets/core.js`): the sentence "I finished the worksheet early because Mr Tan gave us 3 easy questions today.", linked in two subjects 15 days apart, secures `ct1.u3`, the top Critical Thinking statement. Levels, the wheel, the Class grid and its CSV all inherit this.
2. **The teacher's validation barely exists.** The replies in one tap are acknowledgements. A teacher validates a competency only at Confirm Proficient, which needs the whole Upper Sec band first. Self assessment without calibration inflates, most among weaker students (Brown and Harris, 2013), so intent 5 is what makes intent 4 valid.
3. **The loop has never run with real accounts.** One exchange takes two Teams messages and two app switches on each side. Both ends are tied to one device: a link opened on a phone lands in an empty console or log. Whether staff can chat with a student in Teams is question 4 in the Phase 2 request to MOE IT, still unanswered.

### How it was checked
- The docs and the code were read; the student and teacher demos were run in a browser.
- `tests/run.sh` on 5.2: all 34 runs pass (30 suites, the console sweep, three colour sweeps).
- Smaller things seen: the demo's Sharing page is empty for Sam Lee, so the demo does not show the loop that most needs showing; ASK is still a placeholder.

## Harman's decisions (11 November)
1. **Not tested yet.** The school is not sure the Log is ready for testing, and wants to test with a small group as soon as possible.
2. **A teacher must validate wherever there is a claim.** GROW and ACT entries are not written all the time: they come where useful, such as the end of a topic, a unit or a camp. An entry should be substantial, and so should the reply: not a quick reply for the sake of it.
3. **A student should not log a GROW with no competency step.** The Log should nudge the student to make the claim.
4. **The channel may be the school's own Teams.** Beatty has its own Microsoft tenant (`@btyss.moe.edu.sg`). Harman wants to know what it affords, for the Log and for CoCher. Open (see below).
5. **For intent 7, the reader is the subject teacher.** The data is mostly personal, so no cohort view for now.

## What the decisions change (my reading, to confirm with Harman)
- **Securing should rest on the teacher.** With decision 2, a statement is backed when a teacher affirms the claim, and the keyword check becomes a suggestion. The load is bearable because entries are occasional. The wheel could show claimed and affirmed in two inks.
- **Replies in one tap sit badly with decision 2.** Revisit them (backlog A5): an affirmation with advice, tied to the claim, in their place.
- **The weekly machinery sits badly with "only where useful".** The weekly reminder (D1), "Write a GROW this week" on Home, a rhythm dot for each week (D4) and the pilot's count by week (P1) all assume a weekly entry. The trigger becomes an occasion (the end of a topic, a unit, a camp), probably named by the teacher. Revisit.
- **The claim step stays on every entry,** with a nudge when a student passes it without a claim. My earlier reading, that the app had inverted intent 3, is withdrawn.
- **A short form of GROW is not needed.** Length suits an occasional, substantial entry.
- **Signals for lesson design go to the subject teacher,** from what their own classes shared: what the class does not yet understand (R1), what it expects next (W), who followed through.

## Version 6 as advised (not agreed)
Close the loop and add nothing else for students.
1. The teacher answers the claim: "I saw this too", or "Not yet, show me", with advice.
2. A conference in the same room: the teacher responds on the student's device in a timetabled conversation. No transport is needed, and feedback as dialogue is what the research on feedback literacy favours (Carless and Boud, 2018).
3. A teacher's note opens an ACT.
4. Class signals for the subject teacher.
5. One small summary for each student that CoCher can read later, as it already reads a Paths link.

On feedback without a database, the choice is who carries the message:
- **The message carries itself (today):** a link through Teams. No infrastructure, most friction.
- **A borrowed shelf:** a file in the student's own OneDrive that chosen teachers can read and annotate. Not a database the school builds. This is Phase 2, and it would serve intents 5, 6 and 7 together.
- **The same room:** the conference above.

## Open
1. **The school's Microsoft tenant.** Two facts from ICT decide what it affords:
   - Do students hold accounts in the same tenant as staff, with Teams chat and OneDrive turned on?
   - Who can register an app and grant consent: the school's own ICT, or MOE centrally?

   With no permission at all, a tenant shared by staff and students would already give Teams chat between teacher and student, and a Microsoft Form limited to the school that records who submitted, so no email is typed. With one app registration it gives sign in with the school account, the log in the student's OneDrive, and teachers' notes there; the same sign in would give CoCher a real identity and a shared view of a student.
2. **What a small test needs** (my advice): one real exchange from end to end with real accounts before any student starts; a decision on whether students see levels and "secured" while the check is this weak; one class, one or two teachers, one occasion such as the end of a unit; then the think aloud and the pulse already in the README.
3. **Who validates an entry with no subject or CCA teacher** (a camp, service, something else): the form teacher?
4. **GROW's wording for experiences that are not lessons.** The questions are the school's, quoted word for word.
5. **Where Goals and Calendar sit** among the seven intents.

## Reorganising (advice)
Reorganise when a second consumer needs the same fact, and before real data exists. For now, no folder moves and no merged repos. Settle three contracts instead:
- **One owner for school facts.** The subject offering is copied in the Log and in Paths; the staff list lives separately in the Log and in CoCher.
- **One competency vocabulary.** CoCher tracks 6 dimensions on 4 levels; the Log tracks MOE's 9 on 3. "Developing" is the bottom level in one and the middle level in the other.
- **One student summary format** from the Log to CoCher.

Split the Log's code only at the seam Version 6 cuts, starting with the console's script inside `teacher.html`. The boundary to keep: the Log is the student's voice, CoCher is the teacher's eye, and they meet at one interface, not in one codebase.
